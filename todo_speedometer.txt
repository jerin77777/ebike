[!] CRITICAL HARDWARE SAFETY NOTICE:
    Raspberry Pi GPIO pins operate STRICTLY at 3.3V maximum!
    Never connect the EM100 Hall speedometer wire directly to the Raspberry Pi
    without verifying voltage and installing level shifting / voltage division.
    Excess voltage (5V or 12V) will permanently burn out the GPIO or SoC.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PINOUT ASSIGNMENT (Matches diagram.png)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
From your custom Raspberry Pi 4B wiring layout:
  * Hall Speedometer Pulse Signal:  Physical Pin 32 (BCM GPIO 12)
  * Reference Ground:               Physical Pin 30 or Pin 34 (GND)
    (Note: Pin 32 is conveniently located directly between GND Pin 30 and GND Pin 34)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. STEP-BY-STEP ACTION CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[ ] STEP 1: IDENTIFY WIRES & MEASURE VOLTAGE BEFORE CONNECTING
    1. Locate the EM100 Hall Speedometer output wire on your controller harness.
    2. Locate a solid EM100 Signal Ground wire.
    3. Take a Multimeter set to DC Voltage mode:
       - Black probe -> EM100 Ground.
       - Red probe   -> EM100 Hall Speed output wire.
    4. Measure at rest (stationary): Note the voltage.
    5. Spin the driven wheel slowly by hand:
       - Does the voltage alternate between 0V and a HIGH state?
       - What is the HIGH voltage level? (Is it ~5V, ~12V, or open-collector?)

[ ] STEP 2: BUILD SIGNAL PROTECTION CIRCUIT
    Choose the circuit matching your measured HIGH voltage:

    A. If the output is ~5V (Standard 5V Logic):
       Build a Voltage Divider:
         EM100 Hall Output
                │
                ├───[ 10 kΩ Resistor ]───┬───> Raspberry Pi Pin 32 (GPIO 12)
                                         │
                                   [ 20 kΩ Resistor ]
                                         │
                                         ▼
                                Raspberry Pi GND (Pin 30 or Pin 34)
                                         │
                                   EM100 Ground
       Calculation: 5.0V × 20k / (10k + 20k) = 3.33V (Perfect for RPi GPIO).

    B. If the output is ~12V (Automotive 12V Logic):
       - Option 1 (Optocoupler - Highly Recommended):
         Use a PC817 or EL817 optocoupler.
         EM100 12V pulse drives the optocoupler input LED through a 1 kΩ resistor.
         The phototransistor output switches Raspberry Pi 3.3V to Pin 32.
         This provides total electrical isolation from motor noise and spikes.
       - Option 2 (Voltage Divider):
         27 kΩ series resistor with a 10 kΩ resistor to GND:
         12.0V × 10k / (27k + 10k) = 3.24V.

    C. If the output is Open-Collector (switches only to GND):
       Connect a 4.7 kΩ or 10 kΩ pull-up resistor from Raspberry Pi 3.3V (Pin 1)
       to Pin 32 (GPIO 12), and connect the EM100 Hall wire to Pin 32.

[ ] STEP 3: COMMON GROUND & EMI NOISE SHIELDING
    1. Ensure a common ground wire connects EM100 Signal GND to Raspberry Pi GND
       (Pin 30 or Pin 34).
    2. KEEP SIGNAL WIRES CLEAR OF MOTOR PHASE WIRES:
       The 3 thick motor phase wires carry massive high-frequency PWM switching
       currents. Running the Hall signal wire right next to them will inject
       electromagnetic noise, leading to fake/erratic speed spikes.
    3. If possible, twist the Hall signal wire with a Ground wire (twisted pair)
       or use shielded cable with the shield tied to Ground at one end.

[ ] STEP 4: VERIFY SIGNAL WITH RASPBERRY PI
    1. Boot the Raspberry Pi.
    2. With the voltage divider / level shifter connected, measure the voltage
       reaching Pin 32 while rotating the wheel. Confirm it stays <= 3.3V.
    3. Launch the Flutter display application (`flutter run` in `/display` or systemd).

[ ] STEP 5: CALIBRATE SPEEDOMETER (GPS ALIGNMENT)
    The software in `display/lib/raspberrypi.dart` calculates:
        speed_kmh = pulse_frequency_hz × calibrationFactor

    Why calibration is necessary:
    The EM100 Hall speed output is derived from motor pole pairs and controller
    commutation; 1 pulse does NOT typically equal 1 wheel rotation.

    Calibration Procedure:
    1. Lift the bike onto a center stand so the rear wheel can spin safely.
    2. Run the display application in a terminal to observe the debug logs:
       `[Speedometer] Pulses/sec (Hz): <freq> | Speed: <kmh>`
    3. Apply steady throttle to run the motor at constant speeds. Note the Hz:
       e.g.,
         At steady RPM 1:  Pulse Freq = 25.0 Hz
         At steady RPM 2:  Pulse Freq = 50.0 Hz
         At steady RPM 3:  Pulse Freq = 100.0 Hz
    4. Carry out a test run using a phone GPS speedometer (or on a rolling dyno):
       Compare GPS Speed (km/h) against the measured Hall Frequency (Hz):
         calibrationFactor = GPS_speed (km/h) / Hall_frequency (Hz)

       Example Data:
         GPS Speed     Hall Frequency     Computed Factor
         ------------------------------------------------
         20.0 km/h     50.0 Hz            20 / 50  = 0.40
         40.0 km/h     100.0 Hz           40 / 100 = 0.40
         60.0 km/h     150.0 Hz           60 / 150 = 0.40

    5. Open `display/lib/raspberrypi.dart` and update:
       `const double calibrationFactor = 0.4; // Replace 0.4 with your calculated factor`

[ ] STEP 6: END-TO-END SYSTEM VALIDATION
    1. Confirm the Rive digital speedometer needle on the Pi screen moves smoothly
       and reflects the actual speed.
    2. Check the Mobile App: Confirm the BLE/WebSocket telemetry dashboard shows
       `Live Speed: XX.X km/h` matching the dashboard display.
    3. Stop the wheel and confirm speed drops cleanly to `0.0 km/h` within 1.2 seconds.
